This study deconstructs the film *Persepolis* (2007) as a model of a permanent repressive mechanism. While ideologies change, the "Machine" of power remains constant, fueled by external violence and internal fear. 

Methodologically, it combines formal film analysis (Bordwell), the theory of disciplinary power (Foucault), and the concept of exilic cinema (Naficy).

### I. The Geometry of Oppression

Visual minimalism in *Persepolis* is not an aesthetic choice for beauty, but a functional tool. The black-and-white frame, per Bordwell, eliminates visual noise to focus on pure structural conflict. 

Shadow (inspired by German Expressionism) is not a mere absence of light, but the tangible presence of the regime. In key demonstration sequences, the crowd is depicted as a black monolith swallowing the individual. Marjane remains only as a thin white line—the final visual trace of human integrity against the anonymous mass of power.

### II. Inertia of the Machine

The core point of the analysis is the realization that the Machine does not stop. According to Foucault’s theory of discipline, revolutions merely change operators and ideological labels, not the mechanism of control itself. 

In *Persepolis*, we see that panoptic surveillance (neighbor spying on neighbor under threat of punishment) survives both the fall of the Shah and the rise of the theocracy. Power here is reproduced not through grand laws, but through the daily "microphysics" of power—the control of the body, clothing, music, and gait.

### III. Reciprocal Violence as Fuel

Hamid Naficy and his concept of "accented cinema" show that the exilic gaze perceives a hidden symmetry of violence. The sequences of the Tehran bombardment during the Iran–Iraq War are not a threat to the Machine, but an opportunity for consolidation. 

External strikes legitimize internal terror. The fear of missiles transforms citizens into obedient components of the Machine, accepting the loss of remaining freedoms in exchange for an illusion of safety.

### IV. Conclusion: The Verdict

*Persepolis* is not history; it is a survival manual for a system that refuses to shut down. Individual integrity is the only grit in the gears of the Machine. 

If the Machine can dominate a person’s internal space through fear (of bombardment or purges), history becomes an infinite black-and-white loop of oppression.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Microphysics of Power (Foucault):** A conceptualization of power not as top-down decree, but as an omnipresent network of daily disciplinary practices, dress codes, and bodily regulations.
* **Accented Cinema (Naficy):** A theoretical framework describing exilic and diasporic filmmaking characterized by themes of displacement, double consciousness, and political trauma.
* **Geometry of Oppression:** High-contrast black-and-white visual design reducing composition to structural tension between the isolated individual and the monolithic state.
* **Panoptic Surveillance:** A social mechanism of mutual monitoring where populace fear enforces self-censorship and institutional obedience.
