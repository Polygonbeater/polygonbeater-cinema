The present study deconstructs the media satire *Network* as a reference model for understanding the mechanisms by which media, political, and algorithmic marketing systematically generate societal frustration. The text strictly separates the original intent of the creators from the applied psychological interpretation of the digital age. The methodological framework integrates the concepts of the „illusion of control“ (E. Langer), „locus of control“ (J. Rotter), and „uncertainty intolerance.“ The final phase transforms this theoretical framework into applied practice, defining a model of informed adaptation through mirrored tactics (systemic attack vs. cognitive defense) for the individual facing permanent political anxiety.

### I. Author's Intent: Television as the Creator of Reality and Frustration
The film was conceptualized as a warning against the demise of objective journalism and its replacement by the hunt for ratings.

* **Artistic Vision:** The intent of the director (Lumet) and writer (Chayefsky) was to demonstrate that once news becomes a commercial product, it stops describing reality and begins to create it. The film shows that the most profitable commodity is not truth, but anger.
* **The Catalyst of the Narrative:** Anchorman Howard Beale suffers a nervous breakdown on live television and urges viewers to yell out their windows: „I'm as mad as hell, and I'm not going to take this anymore!“ Instead of being pulled from the screen, the corporation monetizes his anger. From the author's perspective, this demonstrates that the system can absorb, package, and sell even the purest form of human despair.

### II. Analytical Interpretation: From Broadcast to Algorithms
When applying the psychology of mass communication to the plot, the exact mechanism of frustration creation (E. Bernays, 1928), now amplified by digital architecture, is identified.

* **The Factory of Dissatisfaction:** The system depicted in the film does not satisfy the informational needs of the citizens; it systematically creates a feeling that citizens are lacking something. The news is designed to maximize internal anxiety.
* **Updating the Paradigm (Bridging the gap):** What the anchorman did in the film broadly to all viewers simultaneously is now done by social networks, tailor-made for each user. Anger is personalized using algorithms. The individual is not locked in their living room with a television, but in their own Echo Chamber, where they are constantly served content that activates their amygdala (fear and aggression).

### III. The Context of Societal Anxiety: Uncertainty Intolerance and the Illusion of Power
The scene where millions of people yell from their windows illustrates the cognitive deficits associated with stress management.

* **Uncertainty Intolerance:** The fundamental engine of political anxiety. The human brain is biologically incapable of tolerating ambiguity; it reacts to it with stronger stress than to a clear threat. Authoritarian and populist leaders exploit this biological weakness by offering a false, absolute certainty („I will protect you, I know whose fault it is“).
* **Illusion of Control:** A concept (E. Langer, 1975) describing the tendency of people to believe they have power over situations they realistically cannot influence. Yelling out the window gives the citizens in the film a false sense of agency, even though realistically they only increase the television network's profits. The system provided a vent that resolves nothing.
* **Locus of Control:** The population suffers from an extremely external „Locus of Control“ (J. Rotter, 1966)—the conviction that their lives are dictated by television and politicians. Frustration becomes chronic because objective control over one's own fate is missing.

### IV. Applied Practice: Mirrored Defense in Zone Zero (Informed Adaptation)
Based on cognitive findings, a model of diagnostics and subsequent treatment (informed adaptation) is defined. A precise cognitive defense is assigned to each systemic attack on the psyche.

* **Systemic Attack:** Generating paralysis from the unknown (Attack on the amygdala).
  * **Cognitive Defense (Predictive Coping):** The subject does not choose the strategy of absolute escape (information avoidance), which only increases the stress of the unknown. They choose analytical immersion. By reading deep (even negative) analyses, they shift cognitive activity from the emotional amygdala to the rational prefrontal cortex. Understanding the situation eliminates panic over catastrophic scenarios and transforms it into a cold preparation for reality.
* **Systemic Attack:** Providing false catharsis and the illusion of control (Yelling out the window / Arguing on networks).
  * **Cognitive Defense (Shifting the Locus of Control):** The subject learns to let go of control where they objectively do not have it (macro-politics, wars). They radically shift their energy exclusively into their Circle of Influence (finishable household projects, local solidarity, sports). By completing real tasks, the brain releases dopamine, restoring the lost feeling of baseline safety and competence.
* **Systemic Attack:** Algorithmic overload with commodified anger (Doomscrolling).
  * **Cognitive Defense (Information Asceticism and Selection):** The subject identifies that a certain source (media, politician) is not broadcasting facts but selling frustration. They apply the grey rock method to digital content. They refuse emotional participation, delete algorithm-driven platforms, and choose highly profiled sources (e.g., specialized podcasts) that do not shock with news but coldly analyze it.

### V. Conclusion: Turning Off the Receiver
The definitive deconstruction of the mechanisms described in the film *Network* proves that modern political anxiety is not a byproduct of the system, but its primary commodity. The system does not require a rational citizen, but a frustrated citizen terrified by uncertainty, who compulsively seeks salvation. Once the individual transitions to informed adaptation—that is, accepts the existence of uncertainty, accepts the limits of their control, and shifts their energy exclusively into the sphere of their own real influence—the media-political algorithm loses its power over them. The true victory over the illusion of control remains the cold-blooded turning off of the screen.

**Glossary of Technical Terms:**
* **Uncertainty Intolerance:** A cognitive tendency to perceive ambiguous or uncertain situations as highly threatening and stressful, regardless of the actual probability of a negative outcome.
* **Illusion of Control:** A psychological phenomenon (E. Langer) where people overestimate their ability to influence events over which they objectively have no power (e.g., getting angry at the television).
* **Locus of Control:** A concept (J. Rotter) expressing an individual's belief regarding who or what controls their life. Internal (I control it) vs. External (the government, fate, or the system controls it).
* **Predictive Coping:** An adaptive psychological strategy consisting of obtaining analytical and realistic information about an impending threat to dampen panic.
* **Doomscrolling:** The compulsive and endless consumption of negative news on social networks, algorithmically encouraged to retain user attention.
* **Echo Chamber:** An algorithmically created digital environment in which a user is exposed only to information and opinions that confirm their preexisting (often negative or polarized) beliefs.
