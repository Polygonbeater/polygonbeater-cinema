Jan Švankmajer’s *Faust* (1994) constitutes one of the most rigorous material rearticulations of the Faustian myth in modern cinema. Drawing on Goethe, Marlowe, and Czech folk puppet theatre, the film abandons both narrative continuity and optical illusion in favor of a procedural system governed by material operations [6][9].

### I. Material Procedures and Cinematic Control

Materiality in Švankmajer’s Faust functions operationally:
* Clay, wood, meat, dust, paper, puppets, and mechanical devices are active components of the film’s governing logic, resisting manipulation and interrupting causal continuity [9][10].
* The film foregrounds its own mechanisms—strings, theatrical machinery, and bureaucratic contracts—rendering power unavoidable through its material manifestation [6][7].

### II. Ideology as Procedure and Tactile Spectatorship

Ideological force and spectatorship operate outside classical models of belief or perceptual transparency:
* **Procedure over Belief:** Faust is fully aware of his constraints, yet agency collapses into procedural inevitability. Ideology operates as a regime of action rather than belief [3][6].
* **Tactile Spectatorship:** Through sound, texture, and stop-motion animation, the film generates corporeal unease, activating an internal imaginative hapticity that destabilizes perceptual comfort [4][8].

### III. Conclusion

*Faust* articulates a cinematic regime centered on tactile governance, shifting film theory from optical focus to material persistence and bodily constraint.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Tactile Governance:** A mode of control operating through material resistance, procedural repetition, and bodily exposure rather than visual persuasion.
* **Imaginative Hapticity:** The internal activation of touch through perception and sensory memory rather than direct physical contact.
