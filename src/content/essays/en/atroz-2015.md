Lex Ortega’s *Atroz* (2015) stands as one of the most radical and uncompromising works in contemporary Latin American horror. Eschewing stylized genre thrills or playful exploitation, the film delivers an unsparing exploration of violence, nihilism, and spectator voyeurism, deliberately confronting the boundaries of cinematic representation.

### I. Formal Strategies: Found Footage and Police Investigation

The film’s formal framework operates as an instrument of direct confrontation:
* By employing a found footage structure intertwined with a police investigation, *Atroz* systematically strips away the safe distance traditionally maintained between viewer and screen.
* Violence is entirely divorced from aesthetic spectacle, irony, or cathartic relief—presented instead in raw, unmitigated terms.

### II. Voyeurism, the Ethics of Gazing, and Nihilism

The thematic core relies on a thorough rejection of moral comfort:
* The film does not merely narrate a story *about* violence; rather, it acts violently upon the viewer's expectations and endurance.
* It interrogates the voyeuristic drive of the audience, forcing a critical reckoning with the ethics of witnessing extreme suffering.

### III. Conclusion

*Atroz* remains an essential touchstone for discussions surrounding artistic freedom, censorship, and the outer limits of modern cinema.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Found Footage:** A cinematic technique or framing device where the narrative is presented as discovered, authentic audio-visual records from personal cameras or official archives.
* **Voyeurism:** The derivation of visual pleasure or obsessive fascination from observing the suffering, intimacy, or violence experienced by others from a protected standpoint.
