> *“Soylent Green is people!”*

Directed by Richard Fleischer and loosely adapted from Harry Harrison’s *Make Room! Make Room!* (1966), *Soylent Green* (1973) stands as a seminal work of dystopian cinema. Released shortly after the Club of Rome’s 1972 *Limits to Growth* report, the film translated contemporary scientific anxieties into a powerful socioeconomic allegory defining the "dirty sci-fi" aesthetic.

## Dystopian Architecture and Class Stratification

Set in an overpopulated New York City of 40 million inhabitants, the film establishes a stark, brutal class dualism:

* **Environmental Ruin:** Permanent smog, intense global warming, and dead oceans characterize a collapsed biosphere.
* **Extreme Segregation:** The wealthy elite reside in secure sanctuaries enjoying rare natural commodities, while the impoverished masses subsist on government-issued synthetic rations.
* **Commodification:** The institutionalization of women as assigned property ("furniture") underscores systemic dehumanization.

## The Corporate Lie and Civilizational Cannibalism

Detective Thorn’s grim discovery that Soylent Green is processed human remains rather than sea algae serves as the ultimate metaphor for societal self-consumption. Having depleted the earth's natural limits, civilization feeds upon itself to maintain a hollow illusion of stability.

> *„Sol Roth’s euthanasia sequence, set against a transcendent montage of lost natural beauty, offers a heartbreaking metaphysical lament for a sacrificed world.“*
