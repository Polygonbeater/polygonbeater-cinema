Robert Wiene’s *The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari* (1920) is universally recognized in film scholarship as the cornerstone of German Expressionist cinema and one of the earliest systematic attempts to subjectivize cinematic space. Horror in this landmark work does not stem from plot events, but from the radical deformation of space, causality, and perception, directly anticipating modern psychological horror.

### I. Anti-Realist Space as Psychological Projection

The film's physical sets are deliberately unrealistic, visualizing inner psychic tension through striking visual distortion:
* Painted backdrops featuring sharp angles, tilted walls, and fractured perspectives construct a world that looks fundamentally "wrong" because the characters experience it as such.
* Siegfried Kracauer interprets this architecture as a symptom of collective anxiety and nascent authoritarianism, where the built environment completely dominates and dictates human behavior.

### II. Mechanical Motion, Temporality, and the Unreliable Narrator

While the camera remains largely static, the rigorous framing generates persistent visual instability:
* Characters move mechanically, governed entirely by a space that functions as an apparatus of disciplinary control.
* Temporal flow is subordinated to a framing narrative. The final revelation of an unreliable narrator retroactively destabilizes reality, turning time into a subjective mental construct.

### III. Allegories of Power and the Instrumentalized Body

Dr. Caligari embodies manipulative authoritarian power, whereas Cesare represents the fully instrumentalized body stripped of autonomy:
* Violence is shown not as an isolated individual pathology, but as the direct outcome of structural domination and systemic control.

### IV. Conclusion

*The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari* establishes an enduring model of horror in which space, time, and institutional power converge into a unified, repressive system.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Expressionist Distortion:** A stylistic approach where sets and mise-en-scène reject objective realism to mirror internal psychological trauma and emotional states.
* **Unreliable Narrator:** A narrative device where the credibility of the storyteller's account is compromised, fundamentally altering the audience's understanding of truth.
