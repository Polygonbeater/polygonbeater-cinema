> *“A non-believer profoundly moved by the sacred, seeking redemption through the act of filmmaking.”*

Before filming *The Gospel According to St. Matthew* (1964), Pier Paolo Pasolini traveled through Israel and Palestine searching for authentic biblical landscapes. Documented in *Location Scouting in Palestine* (*Sopralluoghi in Palestina*, 1965), this journey evolves far beyond a routine technical scouting trip into an intense philosophical inquiry into modernity, faith, and representation.

## Displacement of the Sacred and Cinematic Essay

Traveling through Jerusalem, Nazareth, and the Dead Sea, Pasolini contrasts scriptural imagery with a Middle East scarred by political turmoil and modernization:

* **The Elusive Sacred:** Pasolini realizes that historic Palestine has been too altered by time to reflect the spiritual isolation of Christ’s life, ultimately leading him to shoot the film in the stark terrain of southern Italy.
* **Precursor to the Essay Film:** Blending observational handheld camerawork with Pasolini’s lyrical, probing narration, the film anticipates the essayistic tradition of Godard and Marker.

> *„Sopralluoghi in Palestina stands on its own as an autonomous meditation on geography, politics, and an artist's confrontation with faith.“*
