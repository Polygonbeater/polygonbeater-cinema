> *“A precious window into the formative stage of a legendary screen presence destined to define Count Dracula.”*

Directed by Alfréd Deésy and adapted from Alphonse Daudet’s play *La lutte pour la vie*, the 1918 silent drama *Küzdelem a létért* (widely known as *Leopard*) stands as a significant milestone in early Hungarian cinema. The narrative follows Pál Orlay, an ambitious architect whose ruthless manipulation of women leads to tragic consequences.

## Historical Value and the Surviving Fragment

While the feature film itself is considered entirely lost, the survival of a brief, roughly two-minute fragment offers immense historical weight:

* **Lugosi’s European Farewell:** The film marks the final European screen performance of Béla Lugosi before his departure for America, where he performed under his Hungarian stage name, Arisztid Olt.
* **Magnetism in Embryo:** Lugosi's trademark intensity, aristocratic charisma, and magnetic gaze are already strikingly evident in this rare surviving snippet.

> *„Watching this fragment is not merely witnessing a scene, but confronting a precious artifact linking the European silent screen directly to Hollywood's Golden Age.“*
