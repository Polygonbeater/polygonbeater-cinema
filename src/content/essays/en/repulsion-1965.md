Roman Polanski’s *Repulsion* (1965) stands as a cornerstone of psychological horror, where narrative minimalism yields to a rigorous formal investigation of subjective collapse. As film scholarship emphasizes (Durgnat 1967; Barr 1970; Wilson 2006), horror here is not an external plot event, but a gradual process rooted in prolonged stress and sensory overload.

### I. The Apartment as a Psychological Apparatus

Formally, the film operates as a model of subjectivized cinematic space:
* The apartment of protagonist Carol Ledoux (Catherine Deneuve) functions as a "psychological apparatus" whose physical stability disintegrates in parallel with her internal perception.
* The strategic use of wide-angle lenses, static framing, and spatial fragmentation deliberately disrupts classical continuity and viewer orientation.

### II. Sound Design as the Primary Stressor

The acoustic landscape serves as the primary engine of psychological pressure:
* Ordinary diegetic sounds—ticking clocks, dripping water, cracking walls, buzzing flies over a rotting rabbit—are repeatedly amplified and distorted, turning into aggressive sensory stressors that mirror Carol’s paranoia.
* Following the opening jazz track, all non-diegetic music vanishes entirely, trapping the viewer inside a claustrophobic diegetic world where silence functions as an oppressive weight rather than relief.

### III. Anti-Narrative Stagnation and Bodily Collapse

The film’s temporal structure rejects progressive narrative momentum:
* The cyclical repetition of routine daily actions generates anti-narrative stagnation, sustaining prolonged viewer uncertainty.
* Catherine Deneuve’s performance is strictly minimalist and reactive; her body does not externalize active conflict, but rather registers the steady erosion of adaptive capacity under structural overload.

### IV. Conclusion

*Repulsion* radically redefines horror through the formal mapping of isolation and perceptual breakdown, establishing an enduring benchmark for cinema focused on psychological pressure.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Sensory Overload:** A state where the psyche is overwhelmed by relentless, repetitive sensory stimuli that it can no longer process or filter.
* **Psychological Apparatus:** A cinematic concept where built environments and architectural interiors directly materialize a character's internal mental state and decay.
