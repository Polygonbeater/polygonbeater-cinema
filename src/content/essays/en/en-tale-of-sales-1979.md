*Tale of Tales* (*Skazka skazok*), directed by the Russian master Yuriy Norshteyn in 1979 (runtime 29 minutes), is not just a film, but a hypnotic visual poem. It is frequently voted by international critics as the Greatest Animated Film of All Time, a testament to its profound emotional depth and technical brilliance. Norshteyn completely abandoned Soviet state-mandated Socialist Realism and conventional fairy-tale narration, creating an intimate, polyphonic visual poem exploring memory, childhood melancholy, and the trauma of World War II.

### I. Multi-Plane Glass Animation and the Depth of Memory

Norshteyn's technological and artistic solution represents a groundbreaking chapter in animation:
* **Glass Tiers Without Computers:** Norshteyn and cinematographer Aleksandr Zhukovsky developed a unique wooden vertical apparatus with several horizontal glass sheets placed at various distances from the camera lens, allowing true optical depth of field, dust, water vapor, and natural light.
* **The Image Emerging from Mist:** The resulting visuality resembles a living impressionistic canvas or old daguerreotypes, capturing the tangible atmosphere of buried memories emerging from the subconscious.

### II. The Little Gray Wolf and the Russian Lullaby

The central character and silent observer is the little gray wolf (*Volchok*):
* **Folklore Turned Inside Out:** Based on a traditional Russian lullaby, the wolf acts not as a predatory monster, but as a vulnerable, melancholic pilgrim with large, sad human eyes.
* **Guardian of Memory:** The wolf drifts between temporal planes—observing family idylls, roasting an apple over a fire, and finally cradling a weeping abandoned infant, serving as a symbol of innocence and witness to the 20th century.

### III. Conclusion: Quiet Genius

Norshteyn's uncompromising vision resulted in profound influence, cementing *Tale of Tales* as a masterclass in how animation can move beyond spectacle to become a deeply emotional art form focusing on the quiet tragedy of time and loss.

### Glossary

* **Multiplane Cutout Animation:** A hand-animation technique utilizing glass panes stacked vertically to achieve real depth of field and atmospheric perspective without computer effects.
* **Volchok (Little Gray Wolf):** A Russian folkloric archetype transformed into a melancholic observer and guardian of historical memory.
