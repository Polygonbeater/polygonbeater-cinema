> *“A dream you can feel under your fingernails.”*

Jan Švankmajer’s *Alice* (*Něco z Alenky*, 1988) transforms Lewis Carroll’s classic into a nightmare of dust, wood, and fur — a surrealist excavation of the subconscious. Where Disney offered color and wonder, Švankmajer offers texture and decay. It’s not fantasy — it’s a tactile hallucination.

## A Wonderland Made of Dead Things

The White Rabbit bleeds sawdust, not charm. He’s a taxidermy relic, compulsively winding his pocket watch as if to keep his own body from falling apart.

* **Uncanny Half-Life:** Every creature — built from bones, socks, and broken toys — exists in a state of uncanny half-life. Their stop-motion twitching is grotesquely human, both mechanical and desperate.
* **Persistence of Objects:** Švankmajer animates the inanimate so completely that the film feels haunted by the stubborn persistence of physical objects.

## The Mind Turned Inside Out

Alice (Kristýna Kohoutová) doesn’t fall down a rabbit hole; she crawls through drawers, boxes, and cracks. Each passage feels like a descent into the body’s architecture — memory, repression, fear. Wonderland becomes a labyrinth of obedience, where even chaos obeys invisible, rigid laws.

## The Language of Texture

* **Acoustic Materiality:** Sound replaces speech through creaking wood, snapping scissors, and grinding glass. 
* **Cinema of the Hand:** Every frame is tangible — fur, nails, feathers, dust. You don’t watch *Alice* — you feel it.

## Allegory of Control

Produced in late-Communist Czechoslovakia, *Alice* reads as a parable of individuality under control. Authority takes the form of absurd ritual, endless tea parties, and the repetition of rules no one understands. Every act of curiosity meets punishment; freedom is reabsorbed into the machine.

## Legacy

* Winner of the Fantasporto Critics’ Award (1989).
* Cited by Terry Gilliam, Guillermo del Toro, and the Quay Brothers as a defining influence.

> *“In Švankmajer’s world, imagination is not escape — it’s rebellion.”*
