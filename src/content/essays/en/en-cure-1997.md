This study deconstructs the cult Japanese psychological horror film *Cure* (1997, running time 111 minutes) as a chilling meditation on the fragility of human identity and the suppressed instincts of modern society. The film formally utilizes the structure of a police procedural to follow Detective Takabe, who is investigating a series of bizarre murders. 

All the perpetrators immediately confess to their crimes but lack any motive; their only connection is a random encounter with a mysterious amnesiac named Mamiya. Methodologically, the text focuses on Tokusho Kikumura's clinically cold camerawork, the use of industrial sound design (low-frequency humming, the rhythm of dripping water), and the erasure of the boundary between rational order and the suppressed vertical of madness. The film demonstrates that true terror does not stem from external evil or monsters, but from the terrifying realization that beneath our carefully constructed social mask, there is absolutely nothing.

### I. The Aesthetics of Banality and the Cold Horizontal of Tokyo

The film's visual identity radically resigns from any traditional attributes of the horror genre. Director Kiyoshi Kurosawa stages scenes in the most common, banal spaces of a modern metropolis: sterile police interrogation rooms, empty hospital corridors, abandoned industrial halls, and neat, impersonal apartments. 

This visual emptiness creates a perfect horizontal of alienation. There are no dark shadows for evil to hide in; everything occurs under relentless, cold fluorescent lighting. The camera often remains static, using detached master shots, placing the viewer in the role of a helpless, clinical observer. The murders themselves occur without affect, almost mechanically and silently, as if they were just another automated task in the routine of an alienated world.

### II. Mesmerism and the Unlocking of the Abyss

The film's antagonist, a young man named Mamiya (Masato Hagiwara), represents a perfect ontological vacuum. He suffers from short-term memory loss; he has no past, no identity, and no goal—he is a pure, empty vessel that merely reflects his surroundings. His method does not involve brainwashing people or implanting foreign, malicious thoughts into them. 

Through simple hypnotic stimuli (the flame of a lighter, the rhythm of spilled water), he merely removes social inhibitions and moral imperatives. Mamiya acts as a key that unlocks the suppressed vertical of the subconscious, letting out the frustration that normal, decent people (a policeman, a doctor, a devoted wife) stifle within themselves. The horrifying, broad "X" carved into the neck of each victim is thus not an occult symbol, but the definitive crossing out of human identity.

### III. The Soundscape of Absolute Unrest

The soundtrack in *Cure* is far more sinister than the image itself. Instead of classic musical motifs, Kurosawa creates a constant, subliminal sonic terror. 

The hum of ventilation, the rumble of a distant train, the rotating drum of an empty washing machine, or the continuous, arrhythmic dripping of water function as tools of mesmerism not only for the characters on screen but for the viewer as well. This industrial ambient induces a sense of physical nausea and gradually blurs the line between objective reality and Detective Takabe's crumbling sanity. Silence in the film never means peace; it is merely waiting for the next disruption.

### IV. Conclusion: A Silent Apocalypse and the Assimilation of Emptiness

The final act of the film ruthlessly rejects classical narrative resolution. Detective Takabe (Koji Yakusho) eventually finds and physically eliminates Mamiya, but he does not emerge victorious. On the contrary, the confrontation with absolute emptiness and listening to an old mesmerist recording on a phonograph definitively infects him. 

In the chilling epilogue in a family diner, we see Takabe calmly finishing his meal with a newfound inner "peace," while a waitress behind his back suddenly, without any warning, grabs a knife. The film ends with a quiet, apocalyptic realization: the cure (*cure*) for the burden of human existence lies in the absolute abolition of morality. The social horizontal of rules has been irreversibly infected, and the destruction of the ego will continue to spread, completely unseen, like a pandemic within modern civilization.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Horizontal of Alienation:** A sterile, detached visual environment of a modern metropolis emphasizing internal emptiness and impersonality.
* **Mesmerist Suggestion:** A hypnotic technique employing simple auditory or visual triggers to strip away a victim's moral inhibitions.
* **Sonic Terror:** The strategic use of industrial ambient noise and low-frequency infrastructure hums to induce subconscious unrest.
* **Destruction of the Ego:** The collapse of authentic selfhood and social masking, releasing uninhibited destructive impulses.
