*Dreams That Money Can Buy* (1947), directed and conceived by German Dadaist and avant-garde pioneer Hans Richter, is a seminal work of experimental cinema. Produced in American exile, the film functions as a vivid surrealist anthology and a powerful collective statement of artistic freedom, gathering the voices of European modernists who had fled Nazi persecution.

## A Collaborative Act of Artistic Resistance

* **Director:** Hans Richter (German Dadaist and abstract filmmaker).
* **Genre:** Experimental, Surrealist Anthology, blending live-action, stop-motion, and animation/collage sequences.
* **Concept:** The plot centers on an unemployed man who sets up a business selling ready-made dreams in his rented room. Each dream sequence is a distinct short film created and designed by a different prominent avant-garde artist.
* **The Exiled Collective:** The film's primary significance lies in its list of collaborators, all of whom were major figures in movements (Dadaism, Surrealism) condemned as "Entartete Kunst" (Degenerate Art) by the Nazis:
  * **Max Ernst** (The scene *Desire*)
  * **Marcel Duchamp** (The scene *Nude Descending a Staircase*)
  * **Man Ray** (The scene *Ruth, Roses and Revolvers*)
  * **Alexander Calder** (The scene *Circus*)
  * **Fernand Léger** (The scene *The Girl with the Artichoke*)

## The Animation of the Psyche

Richter masterfully uses the freedom of animation and collage to visualize the chaos, irrationality, and beauty of the subconscious, directly contrasting the Nazis' demand for rigid, legible, realistic art.

* **Dadaist Strategy:** By turning the irrational and the dream state into a commercial product, Richter employs Dadaist satire, mocking the commercialization of the psyche while simultaneously asserting the fundamental importance of the imagination.
* **The Visualization of Inner Conflict:** The various dream sequences—often utilizing abstract forms, stop-motion animation, and theatrical lighting—are visual manifestos affirming that the inner, subjective reality of the human mind is complex, valuable, and inherently resistant to total control.

## Legacy

*Dreams That Money Can Buy* is more than just an art film; it is a monument to solidarity in exile. It proves that the "degenerate" art suppressed and burnt in Europe was, in fact, the vanguard of psychological and creative liberation, cementing the Surrealist and Dadaist influence on American independent filmmaking.
