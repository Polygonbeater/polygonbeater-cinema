This study deconstructs the seminal British folk-horror work *The Blood on Satan's Claw* (1971, running time 93 minutes) as a chilling probe into the genesis of a pagan cult in 17th-century England. The film analyzes the gradual decay of a rural community after a deformed, hairy skull of unknown origin is unearthed in a field. 

The discovery acts as a psychological and biological virus that infects the local youth, who, led by the charismatic Angel Blake, turn to ritualistic murders, the wearing of animal masks, and the worship of a being called Behemoth. Methodologically, the text focuses on the generational conflict, the symbolism of untamable nature (mud and fur), and the erosion of Christian authority. The film portrays rural tradition not as a folkloric idyll, but as a constant threat of regression into animalistic barbarism.

### I. The Open Wound in the Earth and the Horizontal of Mud

The horror here does not come from outer space or a gothic castle; it literally erupts from the soil itself. When a plowman strikes a strange skull with an eye, he symbolically opens a scar in the landscape. Director Piers Haggard visually defines the horizontal of mud and woods—a rural space that is muddy, dirty, and full of decaying matter. 

This soil conceals a pre-Christian, dark power. While the authorities (represented by the arrogant Judge) deny the existence of evil and attempt to maintain rational order, the ancient infection spreads like rot, infecting the younger generation and proving that the veneer of civilization is exceedingly thin in the English countryside.

### II. The Youth Rebellion and the Genesis of the Pagan Coven

The film was made in the aftermath of the late 1960s hippie movement, and its narrative clearly reflects generational conflict. The local youth, fascinated by the discovery and led by the angelically beautiful but psychopathic Angel Blake (Linda Hayden), reject the authority of their parents and the Church. 

They form their own twisted coven deep in the woods. Their "games" begin with ritual dressing up and end in rape and human sacrifice. Angel Blake functions as a dark version of the May Queen—she does not wear flowers, but wields a bloody authority, manipulating the other children to sacrifice their peers to satisfy the entity slumbering beneath the earth.

### III. Fur, Masks, and Physical Mutation

A crucial motif associated with folklore and rituals here is physical transformation. The contagion manifests itself by causing patches of black, animalistic fur (the "Devil's Skin") to grow on the bodies of some teenagers. To hide this mutation, or conversely, to celebrate it, they dress in furs and animal masks. 

They shed their human identity and transform into a pack. The cutting away of the infected skin (in a chilling amputation scene) is a desperate attempt by medical science to stop the animalistic nature demanding a return. The masks here do not function as carnival props, but as a true manifestation of the inner monster that has turned the children into predators.

### IV. Conclusion: Cleansing by Fire and Sword

When the rational approach definitively fails, the brutal vertical of power steps in. The Judge (Patrick Wymark) realizes that this kind of evil cannot be negotiated with, nor can it be cured by theology. In the finale, he discards his judge's wig, takes up a massive sword, and leads the villagers into the woods to physically exterminate the cult. 

The final clash with the partially materialized monster Behemoth is not a battle of good prayers, but a bloody butchery. The return to order is redeemed through radical violence. The spring celebrations are drowned in the blood of children, because nature, as the film constantly reminds us, demands flesh.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Horizontal of Mud:** Visual framing of the rural environment as a decaying, biological mass harboring ancient pre-Christian forces beneath the veneer of civilization.
* **Archaeology of Evil:** The concept where evil is not an abstract or spiritual entity, but a physical relic of a pagan past unearthed directly from the soil.
* **Devil's Skin:** A biological manifestation of contagion appearing as animal fur on adolescents, embodying the regression of humanity into primal barbarism.
* **Brutal Vertical of Power:** The relentless, violent restoration of civilizational order by judicial and state authority regardless of the perpetrators' age.
