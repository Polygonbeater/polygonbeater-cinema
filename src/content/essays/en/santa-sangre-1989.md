Alejandro Jodorowsky’s *Santa Sangre* (1989) stands as a landmark of world art-horror and surrealist cinema. Produced as a Mexican-Italian co-production and regularly featured in retrospectives at institutions such as the BFI and MoMA, the film radically shifts genre logic: horror ceases to be a mere theatrical effect and becomes a symbolic, interpretively open language intertwining ritual, psychoanalysis, and bold visual stylization.

### I. The Circus as a Liminal Space of Power and Identity

The circus occupies a central role in the film's topography, functioning as a liminal site bridging ritual, performance, and violence:
* As a domain of disciplined bodies, extreme movement, and public display, it becomes the crucible where the protagonist’s identity is shaped and trauma is transmuted into permanent performance.
* The circus aesthetic binds control, fascination, and vulnerability together, establishing a precursor to the violent religious cult that dictates the narrative's climax.

### II. Theoretical Framework: Psychoanalysis and Ritual Anthropology

Interpretations of the film draw directly on established academic paradigms (Freud, Lacan, Eliade, Turner, Foucault):
* **Psychoanalysis:** Addresses the return of the repressed, the splitting of the subject, and the suffocating, pathological symbiosis between mother and son.
* **Anthropology and Power:** The religious cult of *Santa Sangre*, sacrifice, and liminality frame the body as both a carrier of sacred meaning and a site of disciplinary control.

### III. Formal and Visual Strategies

Jodorowsky’s stylistic approach constructs a rigorous ritualistic atmosphere:
* Meticulously composed, predominantly static framings and striking symmetry—anchored by an iconographic palette of red, white, and gold—render a mental rather than objective reality.
* Editing pairs classical continuity with dreamlike mnemonic incursions. Music assumes an obsessive, structural function, while silence operates as an active dramatic force.

### IV. Conclusion

*Santa Sangre* redefines art-horror as a symbolic framework for decoding trauma and power, leaving a lasting legacy traceable in the works of directors like David Lynch and Nicolas Winding Refn.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Liminal Space:** A transitional threshold between distinct states or orders (here bridging circus performance, ritual, and psychosis) where conventional social rules dissolve.
* **Pathological Symbiosis:** An unhealthy, enmeshed relationship between individuals (such as mother and son) that erodes personal autonomy and fosters psychological collapse.
