> *“In this city, even silence has teeth.”*

Jan Balej’s *One Night in One City* (2007) is not a film you watch — it’s a fever you drift through. Crafted in meticulous stop-motion over nearly a decade, this Czech masterpiece exists somewhere between urban folklore and psychological horror, between laughter and unease. It is a world built from sighs, shadows, and decaying wood — a place where language has died, and only the grotesque remains to tell our stories.

## The City Without Words

There are no dialogues here, only murmurs, groans, and gasps — the last breath of communication. In this half-rotten tenement world, the inhabitants move like puppets carved from loneliness:

* A man tries to create a living woman from a fish.
* An old lady nurtures an undead pet.
* Neighbors spy, envy, and scheme, while a butcher sharpens knives for meat that seems disturbingly alive.

Each vignette is a fragment of moral corrosion, connected not by plot, but by atmosphere — a slow-motion spiral of absurdity and despair.

## The Body as Landscape

Balej’s stop-motion puppets are both human and monstrous — waxen skin, glass eyes, trembling gestures. Their fragility mirrors the decay of the world around them. Lighting seeps through smoke and dust like a dying candle, and the camera lingers where it hurts, watching as decay becomes routine.

## Grotesque as Empathy

Where horror normally punishes, Balej observes. His characters commit terrible, absurd acts — but never from cruelty. They are desperate for touch, meaning, and companionship. It is a grotesque of compassion: the horror of people trying to be human when humanity itself is gone.

## Legacy of Czech Surrealism

Emerging from the school of Jiří Trnka and Jan Švankmajer, Balej inherits their tactile surrealism but strips away overt symbolism. His city is a living organism — a Kafkaesque ecosystem of damp corridors, secret sins, and flickering lights. This is surrealism grounded in dirt, not dream — an autopsy of modern life through the lens of puppet animation.

> *“Balej’s city doesn’t dream. It decomposes beautifully.”*
