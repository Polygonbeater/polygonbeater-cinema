Fritz Lang’s final cinematic work, *Die 1000 Augen des Dr. Mabuse* (*The 1,000 Eyes of Dr. Mabuse*, 1960), offers a chillingly modern diagnostic of institutional power. Returning to his iconic criminal mastermind after decades in Hollywood exile, Lang eschews nostalgia and expressionist flourishes in favor of a rigorous structural critique of the surveillance state.

### I. Power Without a Subject and the Luxor Hotel

Across Lang’s Mabuse trilogy, the mechanism of domination undergoes a profound evolution:
* **1922 (*Dr. Mabuse, the Gambler*):** Hypnotic manipulation and anarchic individual will.
* **1933 (*The Testament of Dr. Mabuse*):** State-level ideological terrorism and institutional subversion.
* **1960 (*The 1,000 Eyes*):** The total absence of a central will. Mabuse ceases to be a person and becomes a structural condition where control is entirely automated.

The Luxor Hotel—constructed during the Nazi era with integrated microphones, cameras, and secret passages—operates as a micro-model of modern society. Public and private boundaries collapse, and human behavior is regulated entirely by the *possibility* of observation, mirroring Foucault’s analysis of panoptic discipline.

### II. Technical Neutrality and the Banality of Power

The film strips technology of any demonic or supernatural aura. Cameras, recording devices, and data registers are depicted as efficient, morally neutral administrative tools:
* Power no longer relies on fanaticism or charismatic violence; it operates through routine procedure, bureaucracy, and constant logging.
* Both the criminal syndicate and the state police utilize identical technical apparatuses, revealing the formal uniformity of modern administrative control regardless of who holds the mandate.

### III. Film Form as Argument

Lang’s directorial style is disciplined, austere, and clinical. Relying on static compositions, mediated viewpoints through lenses and monitors, and systematic editing, the film actively integrates the viewer into the scopic regime, aesthetically enacting normalized surveillance.

### IV. Conclusion

Lang’s final cinematic statement is profoundly sobering: the most dangerous form of authority requires no tyrant. It is entirely functional, internalized, and banal.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Panopticism:** A structural model of discipline analyzed by Michel Foucault wherein constant potential visibility forces subjects to internalize self-regulation.
* **Power Without a Subject:** Tom Gunning’s formulation describing a decentralized system of control operating independently of a charismatic personal origin.
