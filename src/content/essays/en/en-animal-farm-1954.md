> *“All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.”*

The 1954 animated feature *Animal Farm*, directed by John Halas and Joy Batchelor, stands as Britain's first animated feature film and a bold, adult-oriented critique of totalitarianism. Adapting George Orwell's stinging novella, it depicts farm animals successfully overthrowing their human masters, only to descend swiftly into a pig-led tyranny under Napoleon.

## Allegories and Aesthetics

The film features a dark, realistic animation style that powerfully underscores its grim themes:

* **Key Parallels:** Old Major as Lenin/Marx, Napoleon as Stalin, Snowball as Trotsky.
* **Central Theme:** The corruption of absolute power and the tragic failure of utopian dreams.

## The Controversial CIA Twist

*Animal Farm* is a notorious artifact of cultural warfare, secretly financed by the American CIA via a front organization. This funding came with a critical condition: a heavily altered, optimistic ending. 

Unlike Orwell's bleak, cyclical conclusion where the animals cannot distinguish the pigs from the men, the film culminates in a successful second animal revolt that overthrows Napoleon. This provided a propagandist message of false hope that internal revolution against Communist leaders was possible.

> *“Pioneering the use of feature animation for serious social commentary, Animal Farm transformed abstract politics into a chilling cinematic experience.”*
