This study deconstructs Andrzej Żuławski's psychological horror *Possession* (1981, running time 124 minutes) as the most extreme cinematic portrayal of the breakdown of a marital relationship. The film analyzes the destruction of Mark and Anna's union, which escalates from verbal arguments to physical self-mutilation and the creation of a Lovecraftian monster. 

Methodologically, the text focuses on Bruno Nuytten's kinetic, aggressive camerawork, the symbolism of divided Berlin during the Cold War, and the unprecedented, physically exhausting performance of Isabelle Adjani. The film demonstrates that when emotional pain exceeds the limits of human endurance, psychological disintegration inevitably materializes into flesh, blood, and madness.

### I. The Geometry of the Divided City and the Alienated Horizontal

The story is set in 1980s West Berlin, in the immediate vicinity of the Berlin Wall. This radical visual setting is not accidental. The Wall functions as a perfect macro-metaphor for the micro-conflict inside the apartment of Mark (Sam Neill) and Anna (Isabelle Adjani). 

The city is divided, filled with concrete, cold colors, and armed guards, creating a horizontal of permanent tension. There is no escape space here, only claustrophobic isolation and the omnipresent threat of destruction. The physical barrier mirrors the absolute impossibility of communication between the spouses, whose words have lost their meaning and turned into mere tools for mutual wounding.

### II. The Kinetic Camera and the Eruption of the Suppressed Vertical

The visual style of the film rejects static observation. Bruno Nuytten's camera is in constant, often manic motion, mirroring the psychological state of the characters. Pans, circling shots, and tight close-ups of deformed faces drag the viewer directly into the center of the hysteria. 

The film's most famous scene—Anna's hysterical fit in a deserted subway tunnel, accompanied by the discharge of blood and milk—represents the absolute eruption of the suppressed vertical. It is not a classic demonic possession, but a miscarriage of the remnants of her sanity and faith. It is the moment when the human body cannot contain the volume of psychological agony and physically explodes.

### III. The Monster as the Embodiment of the Vacuum and the Doppelgänger

Anna addresses her emotional coldness and sexual frustration from a dysfunctional marriage not merely through a standard affair (with the eccentric Heinrich). In an abandoned apartment, she secretly feeds and tends to a tentacled monster, born of blood and bodily fluids. This creature is neither an alien nor a supernatural demon; it is the literal materialization of her emptiness and hatred. 

The monster gradually transforms and absorbs a human shape until it becomes Mark's perfect, yet emotionally submissive and cold double (doppelgänger). Simultaneously, Mark meets Helen, his son's teacher, who is physically identical to Anna but lacks her neurotic destructiveness. Synthetic copies replace the originals.

### IV. Conclusion: The Apocalypse of the Ego and the Victory of Synthesis

The final act of the film represents the definitive destruction of human identity. The real Mark and the real Anna die on a staircase in a pool of blood, completely destroyed by their own relationship. However, their created doubles take over the world. 

The final shot, where the synthetic Mark enters the apartment while apocalyptic sirens sound outside and a dead-eyed Helen refuses to open the door, is a triumph of complete nihilism. The disintegration is complete. Human beings unable to bear the weight of their own emotions have been eradicated and replaced by their perfect, empty versions, over which the curtain of global ruin falls.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Materialization of Pain:** The physical manifestation of psychological agony and emotional vacuum into living biological tissue.
* **Kinetic Camera:** An aggressive, constantly moving camera style reflecting the psychotic tension and emotional state of characters.
* **Synthetic Doppelgänger:** A substitute copy created out of emotional emptiness to replace the emotionally destroyed original human being.
* **Apocalypse of the Ego:** The complete dissolution of human identity and sanity under the pressure of intolerable interpersonal trauma.
