This study deconstructs Duncan Jones's existential sci-fi thriller *Moon* (2009, running time 97 minutes) as a masterpiece of psychological horror. The film analyzes the story of astronaut Sam Bell, who solitarily oversees the mining of helium-3 on the far side of the Moon and is nearing the end of his three-year shift. 

Methodologically, the text focuses on the claustrophobic design of the lunar base, the loss of identity through the revelation of his own cloning, and the concept of absolute corporate indifference. The film illustrates the most terrifying aspect of space exploration: we are not met by an alien monster, but by the horror that, to the system back on Earth, we are merely replaceable biological components with falsified memories.

### I. Lunar Isolation and the Horizontal of Emptiness

While real missions (like Artemis) are full of communication with mission control, Sam Bell (Sam Rockwell) is completely cut off on the Sarang base. Due to an alleged satellite malfunction, communication with Earth occurs only via pre-recorded messages. Director Duncan Jones builds a dense horizontal of isolation here. 

The base is a sterile, white prison surrounded by the endless gray wasteland of the lunar surface. Sam's only companion is the artificial intelligence GERTY. The horror in the first third of the film stems not from a direct threat, but from the crushing weight of loneliness, the disruption of circadian rhythms, and the gradual loss of sanity in an environment unsuited for the human psyche.

### II. The Trauma of the Mirror and Implanted Identity

True psychological terror ensues when Sam, following an accident out on the surface, encounters an exact copy of himself—another Sam Bell. The revelation that he is not a unique human being, but merely one of many clones with a manufactured lifespan (designed to break down and die after three years), is a devastating deconstruction of the ego. 

Everything that makes Sam human—his memories of his wife Tess, his desire to see his daughter, his dream of returning home—is revealed to be mere implanted software. It is the ultimate loss of identity; Sam is robbed even of his own past, and his deepest human emotions are merely tools to ensure his operational compliance.

### III. The Banality of Corporate Evil

Unlike in *2001: A Space Odyssey*, the artificial intelligence GERTY (voiced by Kevin Spacey) does not function as a malicious antagonist here. GERTY genuinely tends to Sam and cares for him within the parameters of its programming. The true monster in the film is the faceless, invisible corporation, Lunar Industries, back on Earth. 

The decision to clone one astronaut and continuously kill and replace him is made without any malice—it is simply the most cost-effective solution for resource extraction. This economic pragmatism represents a chilling banality of evil that resonates with the viewer far more strongly than any sci-fi villain.

### IV. Conclusion: Earth as the Source of Betrayal

The sight of planet Earth in the night sky is often a symbol of hope and home in classic sci-fi. In *Moon*, however, this perspective is radically inverted. Earth no longer means home for Sam. It is the place where the real Sam Bell lives, having long ago sold his DNA and memories to the corporation, and the place from which Sam's slavery is orchestrated. 

When one of the clones desperately attempts to send evidence of these crimes back to Earth at the end, it is not an act of triumph, but the self-preservation instinct of a betrayed machine made of flesh and bone. The film presents the chilling realization that in space, we must fear most what we have brought with us from home.

### Glossary and Bibliography

* **Lunar Isolation:** A psychological state of confinement in a hostile extraterrestrial environment without real-time contact.
* **Memory Cloning:** The implantation of fabricated memories to manipulate and control cloned subjects.
* **Banality of Corporate Evil:** Systemic exploitation and enslavement of human life driven purely by economic pragmatism.
* **Trauma of the Mirror:** Psychological collapse triggered by confronting one's clone and losing belief in one's uniqueness.
