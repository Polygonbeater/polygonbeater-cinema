> *“Often hailed as the world’s first horror film, Méliès’ The House of the Devil doesn't just predate cinema; it invents the supernatural on screen!”*

Georges Méliès’ *Le Manoir du Diable* (*The House of the Devil*, 1896) stands as a monumental milestone. Lasting just over three minutes, it is a breathtaking whirlwind of early cinematic magic: bats transforming into demons, skeletons appearing in bursts of smoke, and the Devil himself outsmarted and vanishing in a final puff of light.

## The Invention of Movie Magic

What makes it extraordinary isn't modern terror, but sheer invention. Méliès didn't just pioneer visual effects; he defined the language of horror, fantasy, and cinema:

* **Groundbreaking Effects:** Pioneered substitution splices and multiple exposures to create seamless illusions of supernatural transformation.
* **Theatrical Roots:** Translating stage magic into a brand-new visual medium.

> *“More than a century later, this three-minute marvel remains an essential glimpse into the birth of movie magic.”*
