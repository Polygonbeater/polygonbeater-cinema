The 1930 Latvian silent feature Lāčplēsis (The Bear-Slayer), directed by Aleksandrs Rusteiķis, represents a unique synthesis of Latvian epic folklore and 20th-century history within the interwar Baltic context. The film was not merely entertainment; it was an ambitious effort to define the Latvian national identity at a time when newly won independence was viewed as an opportunity for emancipation after centuries of foreign rule.

### 1. Myth as Legitimate History
Unlike films produced within the Soviet sphere, which were subordinated to narratives of "historical progress" and class struggle, Lāčplēsis turns toward ancient roots. The film parallels the destiny of the legendary hero Lāčplēsis with the story of a young Latvian officer fighting in 1919. This narrative structure sends a clear message: the Latvian struggle for freedom is not an accidental event of the 20th century, but the fulfillment of an ancient national destiny. The film thus presents a powerful counter-narrative to imperial interpretations that reduced the Baltics to mere "lost provinces" of the empire.

### 2. Visual Symbolism of Freedom
Rusteiķis’s film is fascinating in its visual vision. It employs monumental compositions meant to evoke the nation's resilience. While Soviet cinema of the period began to unify its visual style in the "service of the collective," Lāčplēsis celebrates individual heroism, honor, and national integrity. For the Latvian audience of the time, it was a visual affirmation: "We are a nation with our own myths, our own history, and our own future, choosing an independent cultural and symbolic trajectory away from Moscow."

### 3. The Archival Artifact as Testimony
Lāčplēsis was marginalized following the Soviet occupation in 1940. The film, which elevated Latvian independence to the status of classical myth, was undesirable in the eyes of Soviet ideologues because it kept national memory alive outside the official framework. Its long journey to restoration in the 21st century is not just a technical triumph, but also the return of forbidden testimony about an independent Latvia to the public sphere.
