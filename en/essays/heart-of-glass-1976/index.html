Heart of Glass (1976): The Somnambulistic Prophecy

Movie🎬 Heart of Glass
Director👤 Werner Herzog
Year📅 1976
Read⏱️ ~4 min
Educational material

An analytical study of Werner Herzog's visionary work exploring hypnosis as a state of being, the alchemy of Ruby Glass, and apocalypse in red grain.

This study deconstructs Werner Herzog’s visionary work Heart of Glass (Herz aus Glas, 1976, running time 94 minutes) as a radical exploration of social collapse and spiritual decay. Set in an 18th-century Bavarian glassblowing village, the film follows the disintegration of a community after the loss of the recipe for precious “Ruby Glass.”

Methodologically, the text focuses on Herzog’s unique use of hypnosis on nearly the entire cast, the visual inspiration from German Romanticism (Caspar David Friedrich), and the figure of the seer Hias as a mediator between reality and myth. The film demonstrates that when the technical foundation of the world breaks, a plunge into an irrational abyss follows.

I. Somnambulism as an Aesthetic Code

The film’s visual and atmospheric identity is defined by Herzog’s decision to place almost all the actors into a deep hypnotic trance before each take. This process created a unique, somnambulistic performance style—characters move rigidly, speak with unnatural pauses, and their gazes seem to look “through” reality.

This method is not merely a directorial whim; it is a visual representation of a collective trance of a society that has lost its purpose. The actors do not represent characters but rather archetypes wandering in the fog of their own subconscious.

II. The Alchemy of Ruby Glass and the Fragility of Order

The mythical Ruby Glass represents more than just a commercial item in the narrative; it is the ontological centerpiece of the village. Its production requires a secret that vanished with the death of the master glassblower.

This loss triggers a process of regression: rational craft (the world’s horizontal) is replaced by frantic searching, rituals, and violence. Glass, as a material, serves here as a symbol of civilization itself—it is transparent, beautiful, but immensely fragile. Once its recipe is broken, the moral and social integrity of the entire community shatters.

III. Landscape as an Inner State

Cinematographer Jörg Schmidt-Reitwein created visual compositions that directly cite the canvases of Caspar David Friedrich. The landscape in Herzog’s rendition is not a mere background but an active protagonist.

Mists rolling over mountain ridges and dark interiors of glassworks lit only by the glow of furnaces create a chiaroscuro that mirrors the internal darkness of the characters. Nature here is an indifferent, majestic witness to human commotion, emphasizing the smallness and transience of human endeavors.

IV. Conclusion: Hias’s Prophecy and the Fall into Modernity

The character of the seer Hias (Josef Bierbichler) stands outside the hypnotic trance of the others. He alone sees the impending catastrophe—not just the demise of the village, but the arrival of a modern age of wars and technological sterility.

The final epilogue on the island of Skellig Michael, where men try to discover if the world is round, elevates the film to a level of a universal myth about the human thirst for knowledge, which is always accompanied by the risk of falling. Heart of Glass remains a chilling meditation on the fact that humanity is only a step away from an abyss where words lose meaning and only a mute, glass silence remains.

Glossary and Bibliography

  • Somnambulistic Acting: The technique of placing actors under deep hypnosis, resulting in rigid movements and detached delivery representing a collective societal trance.
  • Ruby Glass: A mythical material serving as the community’s ontological centerpiece, whose loss triggers social regression and moral collapse.
  • Romantic Chiaroscuro: Visual composition inspired by C. D. Friedrich’s paintings, where the landscape acts as a character reflecting the internal darkness of the figures.
  • Hias’s Prophecy: An apocalyptic vision from a seer outside the hypnotic trance, predicting the fall into an age of war and technological sterility.
